Rainfall anomaly overview

Recent rainfall in each Nigerian state compared with the 1991–2020 average for the same time of year.

Rainfall-anomaly indicator, not a flood forecast. Flags show where recent satellite-and-station rainfall estimates are well above normal. They do not account for river levels, dam releases, soil saturation, drainage or exposure, and the thresholds have not been validated against flood records. Read the methods and limitations.

Methods and limitations

What this page calculates, and what it cannot tell you

What is calculated

  1. Recent rainfall. CHIRPS v3.0 rainfall estimates for the last three dekads (10-day periods, about one month) are added together and averaged over each state’s area.
  2. Normal rainfall. The same three calendar dekads are totalled for each year from 1991 to 2020, and those 30 totals are averaged.
  3. Comparison. Percent of normal = recent ÷ normal × 100. Anomaly = recent − normal, in millimetres.
  4. Flag. Assigned from the rules in the table. Both conditions must be met. The millimetre condition prevents dry-season false alarms, where a few millimetres can be a large percentage.
Flag% of normaland anomaly

These thresholds are illustrative design values chosen to be easy to explain. They have not been calibrated against Nigerian flood records (see dissertation Section 4.9).

Limitations

  • Not a flood forecast. Flooding also depends on river flow, upstream rainfall (including outside Nigeria), dam operations, soil saturation, topography, drainage and exposure. None of these are included.
  • Not validated. The flags have not been tested for how often they coincide with real floods (hit rate or false alarms).
  • Looks back, not ahead. It summarises rainfall that has already fallen. It does not predict future rainfall.
  • Coarse in space and time. State averages hide local extremes, and dekadal totals hide short intense storms that drive flash flooding. CHIRPS grid cells are about 5.5 km across.
  • Preliminary data. The newest dekads often come from the preliminary CHIRPS product, which has fewer quality checks and can change when the final product is released.
  • Estimates, not gauges. CHIRPS blends satellite data with station data. Station coverage in parts of Nigeria is sparse.
  • No user testing. The interface follows the DGARI design specification (Appendix A) but has not been evaluated with users.
